This hands-on lab uses the U.S. Department of Labor's [O*NET OnLine](https://www.onetonline.org/) to turn career aspirations into data. You will research a real occupation, assess its outlook, and leave with a draft that directly feeds your [Professional Goals + Three-Year Plan](/courses/dmd-4025/assignments/professional-goals/) assignment.

<Aside type="caution" title="Complete this lab BEFORE Professional Goals">
Your Professional Goals statement requires you to answer: *What are your current strengths? What skills must you acquire? What will you achieve this year / in this course / in 3 years?* This lab gives you the evidence to answer those questions credibly.
</Aside>

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lab you will be able to:

- Locate any occupation on O*NET OnLine by keyword or SOC code
- Extract and interpret **Tasks, Technology Skills, Job Zone/Education, and Wages & Employment** data
- Evaluate **Bright Outlook**, projected growth, and openings to assess industry health
- Identify 2–3 skill gaps and translate them into actionable steps for Years 1–3

## What to Bring

- Laptop
- 2–3 dream job titles (e.g., "UX Designer," "Motion Graphics Artist," "Web Developer")
- Your DMD concentration (or the one closest to your goal)

## Part 1: Find Your Occupation (10 min)

1. Go to **[onetonline.org](https://www.onetonline.org/)** → **Occupation Keyword Search** at the top.
2. Type a title (e.g., *Web Developer*) or SOC code. Try the DMD starter codes below if you are unsure.
3. Open the occupation → click **Details** (not Summary) for the full report. Save the URL — you will cite it.

## Part 2: Guided Research — Complete the Worksheet (20 min, individually)

Work solo. Fill every row with *specific* data, not generics. Keep the Details page open and scroll to each anchor link.

### Research Worksheet

Copy this table into your notes, a Google Doc, or print it. You will submit it with your Professional Goals draft.

| # | O*NET Section (anchor) | What to Record — Be Specific |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Occupation Title + SOC + URL** | e.g., *15-1255.00 Web & Digital Interface Designers* — paste full `onetonline.org/link/details/...` URL |
| **B** | **Tasks** `#Tasks` | Top 3 **Core** tasks (Importance score) + 1 **Supplemental** task. Quote or paraphrase. |
| **C** | **Technology Skills** `#TechnologySkills` | List 5 **Hot Technologies / Software** (e.g., Figma, Adobe After Effects, React, Maya, Blender). Star `*` any you have not used. |
| **D** | **Knowledge + Skills + Abilities** | 3 gaps: for each, write `HAVE` vs `NEED TO ACQUIRE` (e.g., "Knowledge: Design — HAVE: color theory, NEED: design systems") |
| **E** | **Job Zone** `#JobZone` + **Education** `#Education` + **Training & Credentials** | Zone (1–5), typical education level, related work experience, on-the-job training, any licenses/certs listed |
| **F** | **Wages & Employment** `#WagesEmployment` | National median annual wage, CT/metro median if available (use **Wages** table → State filter), **Bright Outlook** tag (yes/no + why: Rapid Growth / Numerous Openings / New & Emerging), **Projected Growth 2023–33** % + **Projected Job Openings** (annual) — is the market saturated or growing? Summarize in 1 sentence. |
| **G** | **Work Context + Work Activities** | 2–3 realities that surprised you (e.g., "Spend time sitting: continually," "Time pressure: strict deadlines," "Freedom to make decisions: some freedom") |
| **H** | **Related Occupations** `#RelatedOccupations` | 2 alternatives you would consider (with SOCs). These become your backup plan. |

<Aside type="tip" title="How to read Wages & Employment">
- **Median wage** = half earn more, half less. Note both *hourly* and *annual*.
- **Bright Outlook** = flagged at top of Summary. If present, it means *Rapid Growth* (much faster than average), *Numerous Openings*, or *New & Emerging*.
- **Projected Growth** comes from BLS; under 5% is slow, 5–9% average, 10%+ fast. Compare to DMD fields.
- Switch **State/Area** dropdown to **Connecticut** to ground your plan geographically.
</Aside>

## Part 3: Concentration Huddle (15 min)

1. **Gather** with your DMD concentration (or closest match if your target is outside DMD). Bring your laptop/worksheet.
2. **Round-robin (2 min each):** Share A–F from your worksheet. It is okay if two people chose the same occupation — compare what each noticed.
3. **Aggregate** on one shared doc/slide (10 min). Include *at minimum*:

   - All profession names + SOC codes researched
   - Salary range (low–median–high) + CT vs national note
   - Outlook synthesis: growing / stable / declining? Evidence (growth % + openings + Bright Outlook)
   - 3 common skill gaps for your concentration
   - 1 concrete idea: *How could DMD better prepare students for employment in this concentration?*

4. **Report out (5 min per concentration):** One speaker per group presents the aggregate to the class.

## Part 4: Draft Your 3-Year Plan (15 min — start in class, finish for homework)

Use your worksheet to answer the five prompts from Professional Goals. Write in complete sentences — this draft becomes your assignment's 3-year plan section.

Copy this scaffold:

> **Where I see myself in 3 years (1–2 sentences, cite O*NET):**
> In 3 years I aim to be a [Title, SOC] working in [industry/location]. O*NET shows this occupation has a median wage of [$X] in [CT/nationally], is [Bright Outlook? Rapid Growth?] with [Y% growth] and [Z openings/year], requiring [Job Zone + education].

> **Current strengths that support this plan (2–3 bullets):**
> - [Strength 1 — link to Knowledge/Skill you HAVE + Tasks you can already do]
> - [Strength 2]

> **Skills I need to acquire (2–3 bullets, from Technology Skills / Knowledge gaps):**
> - [Skill 1 — specific software, technique, or credential] → *How I will learn it: [course, tutorial, project]*
> - [Skill 2]

> **This year (Year 1 — what I will complete by end of academic year):**
> - [Actionable step — e.g., "Build 2 portfolio pieces using Figma + React to address Technology Skills gap; complete internship search via Handshake"]

> **During this course (next 6–8 weeks):**
> - [Actionable step — e.g., "Revise portfolio site to showcase responsive design task from O*NET Tasks list; add case study"]

> **Years 2–3 (entry → growth):**
> - Year 2 entry role (often from Related Occupations or Job Zone ladder): [e.g., Junior UX Designer]
> - Year 3 target: [e.g., Mid-level Product Designer managing sprints — aligns with Work Activities: 'Thinking Creatively' + 'Working with Computers']

<Aside type="note" title="Make it evidence-based">
Weak: *"I will get a good job in design."*
Strong: *"O*NET lists Job Zone 4 for Web & Digital Interface Designers, requiring a bachelor's + portfolio. To close my gap in 'Interaction Design' and 'JavaScript,' I will complete DMD 4025 portfolio revisions by [date] and build one React project by May."*
</Aside>

## Instructor Notes

- **Timing:** If the class is 50 min, assign Part 4 as homework and shorten Huddle to 10 min. For 75 min, keep as written.
- **Setup:** Test `onetonline.org` on lab machines beforehand; no login required (unlike `uconn.firsthand.co` Vault). Have CT wage table pre-filtered for one example.
- **Differentiation:** Students targeting grad school should also note Job Zone 5 occupations and cross-check with Graduate Education pages.
- **Assessment (formative, complete/incomplete for lab; summative via Professional Goals rubric):** Worksheet cites O*NET URL+SOC, all rows specific (not "good salary"), outlook interpreted in own words, 3-year steps are dated and tied to gaps. Common deduction: pasting without interpretation.
- **Accessibility:** O*NET offers CSV/XLSX exports for each table — useful for screen readers and for students who prefer spreadsheet data.
