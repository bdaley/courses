This hands-on lab prepares you to build your [Portfolio Website](/courses/dmd-4025/topics/portfolio-website/). You will compare **hand-coding vs. site builders**, research real costs (including whether a domain is included in hosting), learn to check domain availability the right way, and leave with a tentative decision to post in HuskyCT.

<Aside type="caution" title="Research only — don't buy anything in class">
Do not purchase a domain or plan during this lab. Prices, renewal rates, and availability change — gather data today, decide later.
</Aside>

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lab you will be able to:

- Decide whether to **hand-code or use a site builder** based on your skills, time, and career goals
- Compare platforms on cost, **renewal pricing**, domain inclusion, templates, and lock-in
- Check domain availability correctly using a registrar + WHOIS (not the browser address bar)
- Post a tentative platform + domain decision with 2 draft personal site goals

## Part 1: Hand-Code vs. Builder Self-Check (10 min, individually)

Answer honestly — there is no single right answer. **Hand-coding is preferred if you want to show coding skills to employers** (especially web/UX paths). Using **AI assistance** for hand-coding is allowed and encouraged.

- Can I already build a responsive page with HTML/CSS, or am I willing to learn with AI help this semester?
- Do I have time for custom code alongside my other portfolio work?
- Does my target field reward custom code (web design, UX, creative tech) or polished output fast (motion, illustration, photo, video)?

Write one line: **My lean is ___ (hand-code / builder / undecided) because ___.**

## Part 3: Domain Mini-Tutorial + Research (15 min, individually)

### Why typing a name into your browser proves nothing

A common mistake: typing `yourname.com` into the browser, seeing no website, and assuming the domain is available. **That tells you nothing.** A domain can be registered, parked, reserved, or simply have no site attached — and still belong to someone else.

The only reliable check is a **registrar search** plus a **[WHOIS lookup](https://lookup.icann.org/)**.

<Steps>
1. **Brainstorm 2–3 domain ideas** (e.g., `firstname-lastname.com`). Prefer `.com` for professional portfolios; note 1–2 fallback TLDs (`.net`, `.co`, `.art`, `.design`) if needed.
2. **Search each idea on a registrar** — [Namecheap](https://www.namecheap.com/), [Porkbun](https://porkbun.com/), or [Cloudflare Registrar](https://www.cloudflare.com/products/registrar/). Record for each: **available or taken**, purchase price, **renewal price**, and whether WHOIS privacy is free. Do not purchase.
3. **Look up one TAKEN name on [lookup.icann.org](https://lookup.icann.org/)**. Compare: a registered domain shows registrar, creation/expiration dates, and status codes; an available name returns no match. This is the difference the browser bar cannot show you.
4. **If your first choice is taken**, note your plan: variation (middle initial, discipline keyword), different TLD, or contact the owner? Also check matching social handles before committing.
</Steps>

<Aside type="note" title="Domain privacy">
WHOIS records are public. Choose a registrar with **free domain privacy** (e.g., Namecheap, Porkbun, Cloudflare, Squarespace Domains) so your personal contact details stay shielded. See the [note on domain registration](/courses/dmd-4025/topics/portfolio-website/#a-note-about-domain-registration).
</Aside>

## Part 4: Small Groups — Compare + Cross-Pollinate (15 min)

1. **Gather** in groups of 3–4 with mixed platform picks. Bring your worksheet.
2. **Round-robin (2 min each):** share your two options, domain findings, and biggest cost gotcha (renewal trap? domain-not-included surprise?).
3. **Cross-pollinate:** ask what others found that you missed — templates, video support, export limits, registrar pricing. There is no report-out; the goal is to broaden everyone's options.

You **may choose a platform you did not personally research** if a classmate's findings convince you — cite what swayed you in your exit ticket.
