This hands-on lab gives you class time to watch and research show reels before you cut your own. You will leave with **3–4 inspirations** and concrete takeaways that feed your [Show Reel](/courses/dmd-4025/assignments/show-reel/) assignment.

<Aside type="caution" title="At most 2 from the alumni list">
Find **3–4** reels total. **No more than 2** may come from the **Recent DMD examples** (alumni) list in the [Show Reel assignment](/courses/dmd-4025/assignments/show-reel/). **At least 2** must be reels you discover on your own — ideally matched to your area(s) of interest.
</Aside>

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lab you will be able to:

- Identify what makes a reel effective: pacing, mood, rhythmic editing, and dynamic shape
- Analyze how reels showcase **work process** and **individual involvement** across projects
- Connect 3–4 inspirations to concrete choices for your own reel

---

## Part 1: Warm-Up Watch (10 min)

Watch **at least 1** reel together or solo from the starters below. As you watch, jot down what grabs you: pacing, mood, music, editing rhythm, how work is framed.

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Starter reels" icon="star">
    - [Studio Visuale: web design showreel on Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/120694859)
    - [Josef Atlestam – Showreel 2016](https://vimeo.com/188227031)
    - Curated list of "[The Best Showreels and Demo Reels](https://vimeo.com/groups/showreelsdemoreels)" by Premium Beat
  </Card>
  <Card title="What to notice" icon="information">
    - Pacing and dynamic shape (fast/slow, build and release)
    - Mood (music, color, tone)
    - Rhythmic editing (cuts on the beat vs. breathing room)
    - Work process shown (breakdowns, before/after, sketches to final)
    - Individual involvement highlighted (titles, role cards, shot callouts)
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## Part 2: Independent Research (15 min)

Find your **3–4 inspirations** now. Count any warm-up reel you want to keep, then search for the rest.

<Steps>
1. **Start with your discipline.** Search Vimeo or YouTube for reels in your area (e.g., motion design, 3D/animation, 2D/illustration, video production, web/UX, game art).
2. **Check the alumni list sparingly.** You may use up to **2** reels from the **Recent DMD examples** in the [Show Reel assignment](/courses/dmd-4025/assignments/show-reel/) — no more.
3. **Record as you go.** Copy the worksheet table below into your notes or Google Doc and fill one row per reel.
</Steps>

### Inspiration Worksheet

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Link + creator/field | | | | |

<Aside type="tip" title="Aim for variety with purpose">
Choose reels that fit your own area(s) of interest and show you original ways of showcasing work, your individual involvement, and/or your process — not just the flashiest edits.
</Aside>

---

## Part 3: Pair Share (5–10 min)

1. **Pair up.** Bring your worksheet.
2. **Pitch (2 min each):** share your strongest find — play 30 seconds if you can — and name what makes it work.
3. **Borrow (1 min each):** your partner names one technique from your pick worth stealing (a title card style, a process breakdown, a pacing trick).

---

## Exit Ticket: Post in HuskyCT (finish as homework if needed)

Independently post your exit ticket to the **HuskyCT discussion forum** for this lab. For **each of your 3–4 inspirations**, include:

- **Link + creator name + field** (e.g., motion, 3D, video, web)
- **Alumni list?** Yes/no — confirm no more than 2 total are from the alumni list
- **2–3 sentences on your takeaway:** pacing, mood, rhythmic editing, how it showcases work process, or how it draws from different projects
- **1 line on application:** one concrete choice it inspires for your own reel (e.g., "I'll open with my strongest 5-second clip like Atlestam" or "I'll add role callouts since my best work was collaborative")
