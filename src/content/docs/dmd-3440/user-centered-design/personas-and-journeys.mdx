Two tools help you design for real people instead of a fuzzy "everyone": **personas** and **user journeys**.

## Personas

A **persona** is a fictional but realistic character that represents a group of your users. It's not a stereotype — it's a composite built from real knowledge about who your users are, what they're trying to do, and what frustrates them.

A typical persona includes:

- **Name and photo** — makes the person memorable
- **Background** — age, job, context
- **Goals** — what they're trying to accomplish
- **Pain points** — what gets in their way
- **Motivations** — why it matters to them

Example persona for a bus-tracking app:

> **Marisol, 28** — commutes 45 minutes to work each way. She wants to know when her bus will actually arrive so she doesn't stand at the stop guessing. She's frustrated that the printed schedule never matches reality and she's often late.

The persona grounds every design decision: "Would Marisol understand this screen? Would this feature help Marisol?" If the answer is no, reconsider.

:::note
Personas are based on research, not invention. Your project is a classroom exercise, so base your persona on real observations — people you know, or users you interview briefly. The point is to design for an actual audience.
:::

## User Goals and Motivations

Behind every persona is a **goal** (what they want to achieve) and a **motivation** (why they care). Your app should make the goal easier and speak to the motivation.

For Marisol:

- **Goal:** know when the bus arrives
- **Motivation:** avoid being late and reduce the stress of waiting

If an app feature doesn't serve a persona's goal, it's probably scope creep. This is exactly how you'll decide what to include in your final project.

## User Journeys

A **user journey map** visualizes the steps a user goes through to accomplish a goal — including their thoughts, feelings, and touchpoints along the way. It's a timeline of experience, not just a list of screens.

A simple journey map has:

| Stage | What the user does | Thoughts & feelings | Touchpoints |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Realizes they need to catch the bus | "Running late again…" | Clock, calendar |
| Decide | Checks when the bus comes | Frustrated by guessing | Printed schedule |
| Wait | Stands at the stop | Anxious, no info | Physical stop |
| Ride | Gets on and travels | Relieved, distracted | Bus, phone |

Reading the map, you can *see* the pain points — that anxious, information-free wait is exactly where a real-time tracking app creates value.

## Lab: Create Your Persona and Journey

For your final project idea:

1. **Create one persona** — name, background, goals, pain points, motivations
2. **Create a user journey map** — 4–6 stages showing what the user does, thinks, and feels as they accomplish the task your app helps with
3. **Circle the pain points** where your app could help most

These feed directly into your **project proposal** and your **wireframes**.
