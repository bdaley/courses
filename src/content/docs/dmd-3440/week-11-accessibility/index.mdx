**Accessibility** (a11y) means designing apps that work for people of all abilities — including users who are blind, low-vision, hard of hearing, or have limited motor control. It's not a feature bolted on at the end; it's a design discipline that makes your app better for *everyone*.

## Mobile Accessibility Principles

The core principles map to the W3C's POUR framework:

- **Perceivable** — information can be seen or heard (contrast, text size, captions)
- **Operable** — every action can be performed (large touch targets, no tiny targets)
- **Understandable** — the app is clear and predictable (labels, consistent navigation)
- **Robust** — it works across devices and assistive technology (screen readers)

On mobile, the biggest wins come from four habits:

1. **Labels for everything** — screen readers announce labels, not icons
2. **Good contrast** — text must be readable against its background
3. **Big enough touch targets** — remember the 44 × 44 points minimum from Week 2
4. **Don't rely on color alone** — error text is red *and* says "error"

## Accessibility in React Native

React Native has built-in accessibility support. The most important tool is the **`accessibilityLabel`** — what a screen reader announces for an element.

```jsx
<Pressable
  accessibilityLabel="Add a new task"
  accessibilityRole="button"
  onPress={onAdd}
>
  <Text>+</Text>
</Pressable>
```

Without the label, a screen reader might announce just "+" — meaningless. With it, users hear "Add a new task, button."

Key props:

| Prop | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `accessibilityLabel` | What the screen reader announces |
| `accessibilityRole` | What the element *is* (button, header, image) |
| `accessibilityHint` | Extra guidance ("opens a form") |
| `accessible` | Group a set of children into one focusable element |

## Accessibility Testing Tools

Test like a user with a screen reader:

- **iOS** — VoiceOver (built in; turn on in Settings → Accessibility)
- **Android** — TalkBack (built in; Settings → Accessibility)
- Turn them on and **navigate your whole app blind**: can you complete every task using only the screen reader?

Beyond screen readers, check:

- **Dynamic Type / font scaling** — does your layout hold up at large text sizes?
- **Contrast checkers** — verify text meets contrast guidelines
- **Touch target size** — are all targets 44 × 44 points or larger?

[Apple: VoiceOver](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/use-voiceover-iph3e2e3d1d/ios)

[Android: TalkBack](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/6283677)

## Inclusive Design Strategies

Accessibility is part of a broader **inclusive design**: building for the full range of human diversity.

- **Design for the extremes** — large text, small screens, one-handed use, low vision
- **Don't assume ability** — a user might rely entirely on a screen reader
- **Ask real users** — people with disabilities will find issues your tools won't
- **Treat it as continuous** — improve access every iteration, not once at the end

## Lab: Accessibility Review of Your Final Project

Run an accessibility pass on your final project:

1. **Screen reader walkthrough** — complete every main task using VoiceOver or TalkBack; note what fails
2. **Contrast check** — flag any text that's hard to read
3. **Touch targets** — verify every target is 44 × 44 points or larger
4. **Labels** — confirm every icon and image has a meaningful `accessibilityLabel`

**Deliverable:** your accessibility checklist and revision plan — what passed, what failed, and exactly what you'll change.
