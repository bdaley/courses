## Information Architecture

**Information architecture (IA)** is how you organize the information and screens in your app. It's the answer to: *what screens exist, and how are they grouped and connected?*

Think of IA as a **sitemap** for your app. For a bus-tracking app it might be:

- **Home** — nearby routes and real-time arrivals
- **Routes** — list of routes, each with a detail screen
- **Favorites** — saved routes and stops
- **Settings** — notifications, about

Good IA means a user can predict where to find things. Put the most important content one tap away, and group related functions together.

## Screen Flows

Where a sitemap shows *structure*, a **screen flow** shows *navigation* — the path a user takes through screens to complete a task. It's drawn as boxes (screens) connected by arrows.

Flow for "favorite a route" in the bus app:

```
Home → Routes → Route Detail → tap star → Favorites (confirmation)
```

Flows reveal problems early: a task that needs eight taps and two detours is a task you should redesign. When you build navigation in [Navigation & App Architecture](../navigation-architecture/), these flows become your app's actual screens.

## Fidelity Levels

Wireframes and prototypes come in different **fidelity** (level of detail):

| Fidelity | What it is | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | Rough boxes and lines. No color, no real content | Quickly exploring ideas |
| **Mid** | More structure, real text, still no polish | Checking layout and flows |
| **High** | Looks close to the finished app | Testing with users, showing stakeholders |

Move from low to high as ideas firm up. Don't polish too early — at low fidelity you'll happily throw away a layout; at high fidelity you won't want to.

## Mobile Design Systems

A **design system** is a shared set of reusable components, colors, typography, and spacing rules that keep an app consistent. Every platform has one:

- **Apple Human Interface Guidelines** — iOS design language (SF Pro type, system icons, tab bars)
- **Material Design** — Android's design language (Material 3 themes, color roles, components)

You don't need to reinvent these — in fact, your app will look more native if you *follow* them. As you design, reference them for:

- **Spacing** — consistent gaps between elements (a spacing scale like 4, 8, 12, 16…)
- **Typography** — hierarchy via size and weight, not random fonts
- **Color** — a small palette: a primary, a secondary, neutrals, and error colors
- **Components** — buttons, cards, list items that look the same everywhere

## Lab: Design a Prototype for Your Final Project

Using your persona, journey map, and concept from User-Centered Design:

1. Sketch the **information architecture** — every screen your app needs
2. Draw the key **screen flows** — the main tasks a user completes
3. Build **wireframes** for every screen (low→mid fidelity)
4. Link them into a **clickable prototype** in Figma (see [Prototyping in Figma](prototyping-in-figma/))

**Deliverable:** your wireframes and clickable prototype, shared as a Figma link.
