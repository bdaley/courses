## UX Focus: Designing for Touch

Mobile interfaces are built for fingers, not mouse pointers. Two ideas matter most:

- **Touch targets** — interactive elements need to be big enough to tap reliably. Apple recommends a minimum of **44 × 44 points** and Material Design recommends **48 × 48 dp**.
- **Thumb zones** — most people hold their phone in one hand and tap with their thumb. The bottom of the screen is the easy zone; the top corners are hard to reach. Put frequently-used actions where thumbs naturally land.

## Mobile Interface Conventions

Users already know how phones work. Don't fight their expectations:

- **Top** — usually the screen title and notifications
- **Bottom** — tab bars for top-level navigation (the thumb zone)
- **Back behavior** — Android users expect the back button to work predictably
- **Platform differences** — a back arrow in the top-left is standard on iOS; Android often uses a system back gesture

## Responsive Mobile Layouts

A phone screen comes in many sizes — small, large, and everything between. "Responsive" means your layout adapts instead of breaking. In React Native you'll use:

- **Flexbox** — the layout engine that stretches and shrinks content to fit any screen
- **Relative sizing** — percentages, `flex` values, and `gap` instead of hard-coded pixel widths
- **Safe areas** — padding to avoid the notch and rounded corners
