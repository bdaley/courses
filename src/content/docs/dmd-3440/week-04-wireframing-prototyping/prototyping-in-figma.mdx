**Figma** is the industry-standard design tool for building wireframes and interactive prototypes — and it's free for students. This page walks through the workflow you'll use for your Week 4 deliverable.

## Get Set Up

1. Create a free account at [figma.com](https://www.figma.com/) (sign up with your UConn email for education benefits)
2. Start a new **design file**
3. Use **Frame** to create a phone-sized artboard — Figma has built-in presets for iPhone and Android devices

Every design in Figma lives inside frames sized like the device you're designing for.

## Build Wireframes

A wireframe is a rough blueprint: boxes for content, lines for text. In Figma:

- Draw **rectangles** for buttons, images, and cards
- Use **text layers** (real or lorem ipsum) for content
- Keep it grayscale — no color decisions yet
- Add a separate **frame** for each screen of your app

Name your frames after their screens (Home, Routes, Settings). Clear names make the prototype and handoff much easier.

[Figma: Getting started](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040037313)

## Make It Interactive

Figma turns static frames into a **clickable prototype** with connections:

1. Switch to **Prototype** mode (top of the editor)
2. Click a button or card, drag the arrow to the screen it should open
3. Set the **interaction** (e.g., "On tap → Navigate to")
4. Add back navigation so testers aren't trapped
5. Press **Present** to preview the working prototype

Now you can hand your design to a classmate and say "try it" — and watch where they get stuck. That's the whole point: catch navigation problems on paper, not in code.

[Figma: Create interactive prototypes](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040514633)

## Fidelity: Keep It Low to Start

For this week's deliverable, wireframes don't need to look finished. Resist the urge to pick colors and perfect spacing. The goal is **structure and flow**. You'll iterate on the design through the semester as you build the real thing.

That said, a *mobile* prototype benefits from a few mobile design conventions:

- A **top bar** for the screen title
- A **bottom tab bar** if your app uses tabs
- Real button labels, not "Button"

## Sharing Your Prototype

Figma makes sharing easy:

1. Click **Share** (top-right)
2. Copy the **link** (viewers, not editors, is fine for submission)
3. Paste it into your Week 4 deliverable

Your instructor and classmates can click through your prototype right in the browser — no installs needed.
