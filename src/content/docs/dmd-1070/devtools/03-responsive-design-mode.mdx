When you change the size of your browser window, does your layout hold up? **Responsive Design Mode** (also called the device toolbar) lets you test your page at different screen sizes without resizing your whole browser window.

## Opening the Device Toolbar

| Browser | How to Open |
|---|---|
| Chrome / Edge | Click the **Toggle Device Toolbar** icon (a phone-and-tablet icon next to the element picker) or press `Ctrl+Shift+M` (Win/Linux) / `Cmd+Shift+M` (Mac) |
| Firefox | Click the **Responsive Design Mode** icon (two overlapping rectangles) or press `Ctrl+Shift+M` |

## Testing at Different Screen Sizes

Once the device toolbar is open, you can:

- **Select a preset device** from the dropdown at the top — iPhone, iPad, Pixel, Surface, and many more
- **Drag the handles** on the right and bottom edges of the viewport to resize freely
- **Type exact dimensions** in the width and height fields
- **Rotate the device** with the orientation button (rotate icon)

The page re-renders with every size change. Any media queries you have written will activate and deactivate as you cross breakpoints.

**Try this:** Open real DevTools and enter Responsive Design Mode. Start with a wide viewport, then slowly drag the right handle narrower. Watch the layout change from side-by-side to stacked at 600px — that is the media query activating.

## Media Query Breakpoint Rulers

When the device toolbar is open in Chrome, you will see **media query breakpoint rulers** above the Styles panel. These are colored bars that represent your media queries:

- **Blue bars** — `max-width` media queries (styles for screens up to this width)
- **Orange bars** — `min-width` media queries (styles for screens at least this width)
- **Green bars** — combined `min-width` and `max-width` ranges

Click any bar to resize the viewport to that breakpoint. This is the fastest way to test how your layout looks at each breakpoint without dragging the viewport manually.

## Touch Emulation

When you select a mobile preset (like iPhone or iPad), DevTools automatically enables **touch emulation**. This simulates touch events instead of mouse events — useful for testing:

- Mobile navigation menus that open on tap
- The difference between `:hover` (desktop, triggered by mouse) and `:focus` (mobile, triggered by tap)
- How elements respond to touch versus click

You can also enable touch emulation manually by selecting "Touch" from the device toolbar's touch dropdown.
