A photo straight from a camera might be 4000 pixels wide and several megabytes in size. The web does not need that much detail. Large images slow down your page, frustrate visitors, and hurt your search ranking.

**Resizing** means making the image smaller in dimensions. **Optimizing** means reducing the file size while keeping acceptable quality.

## Lossy vs. Lossless Compression

When you optimize an image, you choose between two types of compression:

- **Lossy compression** discards some image data to reduce file size. The quality loss is often invisible, but it is permanent. JPEG and WebP both support lossy compression.
- **Lossless compression** keeps every pixel exactly as it was. The file size reduction comes from more efficient encoding. PNG uses lossless compression.

The right choice depends on the image. A photograph can tolerate lossy compression because the human eye does not notice small color changes in a complex scene. A logo with crisp edges should use lossless compression to stay sharp.

## How to Resize and Optimize

### Step 1: Choose the right dimensions

Decide the maximum width the image will ever be displayed on your page. For a full-width banner, that might be 1920 pixels. For a thumbnail, 150 pixels. Do not use an image larger than its display size.

### Step 2: Use an editing tool

Desktop tools:
- **Adobe Photoshop** — industry standard for image editing
- **Adobe Express Online** — free browser-based tool
- **ImageResizer** for Windows

VS Code extension:
- **Image Resizer** extension resizes images directly inside your editor

Browser tools:
- **Squoosh** — free online tool that lets you compare quality side by side

### Step 3: Choose the right format

Use JPEG for photographs, PNG for images needing transparency, and WebP for the best of both worlds.
