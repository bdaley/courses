## What is a Site Map?

Or a "site diagram," as defined by [Web Style Guide](https://webstyleguide.com/4-information-architecture.html):

> As your team works out the information architecture and major categories of content, site diagrams visualize the developing information hierarchy and help communicate the organizational concepts to the team and to stakeholders and project sponsors. This communications role is crucial throughout the project, as the site diagram evolves in iterations from a brainstorming and planning document into a blueprint for the actual site as it will be developed.

:::note
**XML Sitemaps**: Before continuing I'd like to point out that the term "site map" can also refer to a type of XML document that search engines use to find the pages of your website. We won't discuss this type of site map, but just know that it exists and you will often find references to the creation of XML site maps when performing a Google search for the term.
:::

## Tools for Creating a Site Map

There are all sorts of fancy tools for creating site maps. Professionally, I used a product called [Slickplan](https://slickplan.com/). It was great for large teams where writers were responsible for content. But most of the time, opening up a simple Google Drawing was more than effective for communicating the hierarchy of a new website. Please use whatever you're comfortable with.

When presented with a new project, our team would "site map" the existing website before creating a new, reimagined site map. This allowed us to easily compare and contrast the changes with a client or stakeholder.
