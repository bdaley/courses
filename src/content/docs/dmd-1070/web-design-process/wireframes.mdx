## What are Wireframes?

Think of wireframes as a blueprint for the design of your website. You've seen [architectural blueprints](https://www.google.com/search?q=architectural+blueprint&sxsrf=ALeKk01eaOZRk6vCAE23sa7lR0CsGKQRsA:1602472661208&source=lnms&tbm=isch&sa=X&ved=2ahUKEwi36-7ti67sAhVroXIEHRUGAnsQ_AUoAXoECBsQAw&biw=1600&bih=1199) before, right?

Ever notice how the blueprints don't contain information about the color of the walls or what type of curtains will go in each room? They are mainly concerned with the sizes of the rooms and how they are arranged within the house.

When we wireframe a web page, we have similar concerns. Mostly, what type of items are on the page and where they might be located. This allows us (and clients/stakeholders) to focus on the content, without getting bogged down with typography or color choices.

## Tools for Creating Wireframes

As usual, I'd like you to use something that you are comfortable with using. (Or are comfortable learning.) This can be [Adobe XD](https://www.adobe.com/products/xd.html), [wireframe.cc](https://wireframe.cc/), [Figma](https://figma.com/), or even [a spreadsheet](https://uxdesign.cc/a-case-for-spreadsheet-wireframes-6e071477db79).
