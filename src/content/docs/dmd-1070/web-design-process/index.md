## Introduction

The first thing you should know about the web design process is that there is no web design process.

What I mean is that there really isn't a standard process that **everyone** follows. A lot depends on your skills, the skills of your team, and the type of web project that you are creating.

Before continuing, please read [Chapter 3 from Web Style Guide](https://webstyleguide.com/3-process.html). While reading, remember that the steps, roles, and responsibilities outlined in the Web Style Guide are only recommendations -- or best practices, according to the authors. Not every team has a U/X Lead. Or perhaps your team does have a U/X Lead who is *also* the Design Lead. Point being, you should find a process that works for you and your team. Iterate and evolve your process. Flexibility is key.

[Web Style Guide: Process](https://webstyleguide.com/3-process.html)

## Our Process

For the purposes of this course, you will function in all capacities and roles of a web team. You are the Project Lead. You are the U/X Lead. You are the Designer. Etc.

The *Web Style Guide* defined a general project life cycle for web projects as follows:

- Site definition and planning
- Content inventory
- **Information architecture**
- **Site design**
- **Site construction**
- Site marketing, tracking, evaluation, and maintenance

We're going to focus on the items in bold.

### Information Architecture

IA is a whole lot more than just figuring out how to organize a website in an appropriate hierarchy. However, our focus is on the more practical aspects of web design. As such, we're going to build "site maps" as a part of our web design process.
